# Liquid Locations — Business Plan

## Executive Summary

Liquid Locations is a premium, tech-enabled production services company bridging London's commercial production market with Spain's cinematic landscapes. Founded by James Huertas — a bilingual British-Spanish production professional with 8+ years of experience across both markets — the company fills a critical gap between expensive, inflexible conglomerates (Palma Pictures, Twenty Four Seven) and local fixers who lack understanding of London agency standards.

The business operates on a lean, scalable model: zero physical assets, variable sub-contracting per project, and a three-tier service architecture (Scout / Coordinate / Full Service) that scales dynamically with each brief. Revenue is generated through fixed day rates, percentage-based markups on crew and equipment, and production management fees — all aligned with APCP industry standards.

**Year 1 target:** 12–15 projects generating €150,000–€200,000 in revenue.

## Legal Structure

### Entity: Sociedad Limitada (S.L.)

**Why S.L. over Autonomo or UK Ltd:**

- **Limited liability** — personal assets legally shielded from corporate debts and on-set liabilities (critical in high-risk production environments)
- **Tax efficiency** — flat 25% corporate tax rate (reduced to 15% for first two years); becomes significantly more efficient than Autonomo above €40K–€60K annual profit
- **Low barrier** — under Spain's *Ley Crea y Crece*, an S.L. can now be formed with just €1 minimum capital (previously €3,000)
- **Corporate credibility** — essential for securing contracts with London ad agencies who expect institutional permanence
- **UK Ltd eliminated** — post-Brexit complications make UK entities unsuitable for hiring Spanish crew, obtaining permits, and reclaiming Spanish VAT

**Registration name:** Liquid Locations S.L. (or Liquid Production Services S.L.)

**Founder status:** Autonomo Societario (company director), paying fixed monthly social security regardless of revenue.

### Registration Steps

1. Obtain NIE (if not already held)
2. Reserve company name at Registro Mercantil Central
3. Open corporate bank account, deposit minimum capital
4. Sign deed of incorporation (*Escritura de Constitucion*) before a notary
5. Register with Registro Mercantil (Companies Registry)
6. Obtain CIF (corporate tax ID) from Agencia Tributaria
7. Register for IAE (business activity tax) — CNAE code for audiovisual production
8. Register founder as Autonomo Societario with Social Security
9. Obtain municipal operating licence (*Licencia de Apertura*)

## Revenue Model

### Day Rates
- Executive Producer / Location Manager: €450–€500/day
- Billed directly for founder's time on every project

### Location Commission
- Standard 20% agency margin on negotiated property fees
- Property fees range: €1,000–€6,000+/day depending on exclusivity

### Crew & Equipment Markup (Tier 2)
- 15–20% markup on local crew day rates
- 15–20% markup on equipment rental invoices
- Covers administrative burden, booking risk, payroll processing

### Production Management Fee (Tier 3)
- 20% flat markup on total below-the-line (BTL) production budget
- Encompasses comprehensive end-to-end management

### Payment Terms
- 50–75% upfront mobilisation fee upon project confirmation
- Balance payable within 14–30 days post-wrap
- UK invoicing: 0% IVA via EU Reverse Charge Mechanism

## Insurance Requirements

All policies typically consume **2–3% of a given production budget**.

### Required Coverage

1. **Civil Liability (Responsabilidad Civil)**
   - Mandatory for third-party bodily injury or property damage
   - Required by municipalities for filming permits

2. **Audiovisual Production Insurance**
   - Cast/crew accidents
   - Media loss
   - Weather-related shoot cancellations

3. **Equipment Insurance**
   - Essential for renting from partner houses (711rent, Palma Equipment Rental)
   - Covers theft and accidental damage

### Estimated Annual Cost
- At €150K revenue: €3,000–€4,500/year in insurance premiums
- Per-project basis: scale policy to specific production budget

## Key Hires Timeline

### Phase 1: Solo Operation (Months 1–6)
- Founder operates as Executive Producer, Head of Locations, and primary client liaison
- All execution sub-contracted per project from freelance talent pool
- Zero payroll drain during downtime

### Phase 2: First Hire — Junior Production Coordinator (Month 6–9)
- Handles logistics: call sheets, transport, catering, accommodation
- Frees founder to focus on business development and client relations
- Part-time initially, scaling to full-time with volume

### Phase 3: Second Hire — Dedicated Location Scout (Month 9–12)
- Continuously expands proprietary digital location database
- Shoots 360-degree and drone imagery across all 6 regions
- Maintains GPS-tagged library as a competitive moat

### Phase 4: Third Hire — Part-time Accounting Administrator (Month 12+)
- Manages cross-border VAT invoicing (intra-community + reverse charge)
- Handles payroll withholding (IRPF) for freelancers
- Ensures rapid payment processing to maintain crew relationships
